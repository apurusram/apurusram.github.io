UX case study

Improving Pedestrian Flow Efficiency at Busy Crosswalks in the Capital

BRIEF

Overview
In the heart of the city of Port-Louis, a major pedestrian crossing connects two sides of a busy four-lane road. It’s one of the most frequently used crossings, especially during office hours, where hundreds of pedestrians rely on it daily to move between commercial and transport areas.

Problem Statement
Currently, the pedestrian flow lacks directional guidance. When the traffic light turns green for pedestrians, people start crossing simultaneously from both sides without a structured walking direction. This results in mid-crossing congestion, accidental collisions, and slower crossing speeds, often leaving some pedestrians stranded when the signal turns red again.

Objective
To improve safety, comfort, and flow efficiency for pedestrians by introducing a visual directional system that organizes movement and reduces crowding during the green light phase.



current flow
  • Pedestrians wait on both sides until the light turns green.
  • Once green, people from both directions start walking simultaneously.
  • In the middle of the crossing, paths overlap with no clear directional pattern.
  • Congestion builds, leading to confusion and occasional contact between pedestrians.
  • Some pedestrians fail to cross in time due to reduced movement speed.
proposed flow
  • The crosswalk is visually divided into two lanes, each representing a walking direction (incoming and outgoing).
  • Clear painted arrows indicate “Walk This Way” guidance for each direction.
  • Optional floor colors or patterns (e.g., blue and white stripes) differentiate the two walking streams.
  • Pedestrians naturally align to their respective lanes when crossing.


proposed solution
Introduce a Directional Pedestrian Flow System using visual design cues:
  • Two-tone crosswalk design: e.g., left side marked for “Going Forward” and right side for “Returning.”
  • Ground arrows and signage: reinforcing walking direction on each lane.
  • Awareness signage: small boards at pedestrian waiting zones explaining the new system.
  • Optional sensor-based timing: can be considered for longer crossings to optimize signal duration.


expected outcome
  • Reduced mid-crossing collisions and congestion.
  • More fluid and predictable pedestrian movement.
  • Shorter crossing times, improving overall traffic efficiency.
  • Enhanced sense of order and safety for both pedestrians and drivers.
  • A model that can be replicated at other high-traffic crossings around the island.
